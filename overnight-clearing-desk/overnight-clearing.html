Game Rules & Mechanics

Objective: Optimize funding costs and maximize net interest margin (NIM) over multiple days while surviving liquidity shocks and collateral constraints.

Starting balance sheet (identical for all banks)

  • Assets: $80B fixed-rate corporate loans @ 6.50%; $20B floating-rate assets (SOFR + 100 bps); $20B US Treasury securities (HQLA).
  • Liabilities & Equity: $90B wholesale corporate deposits (sticky, fixed 3.50%); $20B institutional short-term funding (volatile); $10B equity.
  • Cash (reserves at the Fed) is the residual. Total = $120B.

Setup

  1. The instructor adds one bank per team. Each bank gets a private link to its own bank page; click Student links to copy them all.
  2. The session is saved, so reloading this page reconnects to the same game. End session / new game closes every student page.

Daily cycle

  1. Morning liquidity shock: apply a Market Condition Card, randomize shocks, or hit individual banks. Negative cash = a funding need.
  2. Interbank repo trading: on their bank pages, teams post bids (borrow cash, pledging Treasuries: a repo) or offers (lend cash against Treasuries: a reverse repo). Pending orders show on the balance sheets immediately. When a bid's rate is at or above an offer's rate they trade at once, at the rate of the order that was resting in the book. A bank can only pledge Treasuries it hasn't already pledged and can only lend cash it actually has. Borrowing cheap and lending on (intermediating) is allowed.
  3. Close & SOFR print: the instructor closes the market. Unfilled orders expire. SOFR is the volume-weighted median of the day's private interbank trades (Fed operations are excluded, as with the real SOFR). Any bank still short is funded by the Fed's Standing Repo Facility at the SRF rate, secured by its free Treasuries; a shortfall beyond its collateral becomes an unsecured overdraft at SRF + 100 bps. Surplus cash stays at the Fed earning IORB.
  4. Advance (EOD P&L): one night of interest accrues into equity (and cash): loans 6.50%, floating assets at today's SOFR + 100 bps (or the last SOFR, or IORB if none has printed), IORB on positive reserves, repo interest received or paid, minus 3.50% on sticky deposits. Day count is actual/360. Then every overnight repo matures: cash returns to lenders and collateral is released, so structural gaps reappear and must be refunded the next morning.

Advanced elements (introduce Day 3+)

  • Sponsored repo: post an external order (e.g. "Hedge Fund (sponsored repo)") to add non-bank demand for cash. It has no balance sheet in the game and can absorb surplus cash, pushing SOFR higher.
  • Collateral specials vs GC: designate one bank as holding a special; others accept a lower rate to borrow that bond (negotiated off-platform).
  • HQLA, leverage, and concentration limits as soft constraints the Risk Officer must monitor.

Quote convention: this game quotes from the cash side (a bid is a bid for cash, so higher is more aggressive). Real dealer repo quotes are usually stated from the collateral side. This is a stylized teaching tool. Real SOFR is the volume-weighted median of eligible overnight Treasury repo transactions across tri-party, GCF, and FICC DVP segments, published by the New York Fed. September 17, 2019 remains the canonical stress episode (SOFR 5.25%).
